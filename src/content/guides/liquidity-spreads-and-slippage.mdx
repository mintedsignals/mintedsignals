The price you see on a prediction market is usually the last trade or the middle of the best offers, not a guaranteed price for your order. What you actually pay depends on liquidity, meaning how many shares people are offering at each price. In a thin market, a large order can fill at a noticeably worse price than the one on screen. That difference is called slippage.

## The order book

Prediction markets match buyers and sellers through an order book:

- **Bids**: the prices people are willing to pay for YES shares.
- **Asks**: the prices people are willing to sell YES shares at.

If the best bid is 60¢ and the best ask is 63¢, you can buy immediately at 63¢ or sell immediately at 60¢.

## The spread

The gap between the best bid and the best ask is the **spread**, 3¢ in the example above. A wide spread is a hidden cost: if you buy at 63¢ and want to sell straight away, you only get 60¢.

Busy markets often have spreads of a cent or two. Quiet markets can have spreads of 10¢ or more.

## Slippage

The best ask might only have 200 shares on offer at 63¢. If you want 1,000 shares, the rest fills at the next prices up:

| Shares | Price | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | 63¢ | $126 |
| 300 | 64¢ | $192 |
| 500 | 66¢ | $330 |
| **1,000** | **average 64.8¢** | **$648** |

You expected to pay $630 at 63¢ and paid $648 instead. That $18 is slippage. The numbers here are illustrative; real order books vary.

## How to reduce the cost

- **Check the volume.** Our tables show 24-hour volume for every market. Higher volume usually means tighter spreads.
- **Use limit orders.** A limit order only fills at your price or better, so you control slippage but may not get filled.
- **Split large orders** or trade when the market is busiest.
- **Compare venues.** The same question can trade at different prices and depths on different venues.

## Why it matters most at the extremes

At 95¢ the most you can make is 5¢ per share. A 2¢ spread or a little slippage takes a big share of that. Near-certain outcomes look safe but leave little room for costs.
