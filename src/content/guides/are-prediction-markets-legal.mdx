Whether prediction markets are legal for you depends on where you live and which platform you use. Some countries treat event contracts as financial products, some treat them as gambling, and some block particular platforms. Each platform also sets its own list of countries it serves. Check both your local rules and the platform's terms before you sign up.

<Callout title="Not legal advice">
  This page is a general guide, updated as rules change. It isn't legal advice. If you're unsure, check with your local regulator.
</Callout>

## Why the rules differ

Regulators look at prediction markets in different ways:

- **As financial derivatives.** In the US, event contracts traded on a CFTC-regulated exchange such as Kalshi fall under federal commodities law. <Check>Summarise the current US federal and state position</Check>
- **As gambling.** Some countries treat betting on events as gambling, which needs a local licence.
- **As crypto services.** Platforms that settle in stablecoins can also fall under crypto rules.

## How to check for yourself

1. **Read the platform's terms of service.** Each lists the countries it doesn't serve.
2. **Check your regulator.** Financial or gambling regulators often publish warnings about unlicensed platforms.
3. **Don't use a VPN to get around restrictions.** It usually breaks the platform's terms and can get your account frozen.
